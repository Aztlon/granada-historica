# M10.5 — Evidence preparation for deferred analytical geometry

Status: **inputs catalogued; no geometry created**
Updated: 2026-10-02

This package prepares the evidence needed for four analytical layers that remain
deferred in M10.5:

1. parish jurisdictions;
2. the historical alignments of Plaza de Bib-Rambla, Campo del Príncipe, and
   Plaza Nueva;
3. a household, property, fiscal, parish, and habices corpus for population
   geography; and
4. a composite citywide extent.

The canonical register is
`data/research/c1550/m10.5-evidence-preparation.json`. It is generated by
`scripts/assemble-m10-5-evidence-preparation.ts`, validated with the rest of the
research package, linked from the c. 1550 manifest, and imported by neither the
public nor internal application.

## Boundary of this tranche

This tranche records sources, target repositories, gaps, biases, derivation
steps, uncertainty rules, and readiness gates. It contains no GeoJSON,
coordinates, bounding boxes, inferred boundaries, demographic surfaces, or
closed space envelopes.

The distinction between evidence states is mandatory:

- `registered_source`: a source record is already present in `data/sources.json`;
- `repository_target`: a relevant collection or document class is known, but
  the exact units have not yet been located and cited; and
- `not_located`: the required evidence class is specified, but no adequate
  source or archival unit has been selected.

An archival finding aid is not the underlying document. For example, the
Archivo Municipal's index establishes a route into the Actas Capitulares, but
claims must ultimately cite the individual act, folio, and signatura.

## Parish jurisdictions

The target set now covers the Salvador / former Albaicín Great Mosque,
San Miguel Bajo, San Cristóbal, San José, San Juan de los Reyes, San Matías, and
San Luis. This expands the preparation scope beyond the four entries originally
named by the analytical-layer placeholder, but it does not assert that their
territories have been reconstructed.

Institutional histories can establish a foundation, conversion, building site,
or campaign of works. They cannot establish a jurisdictional boundary. The next
documentary work is therefore to locate:

- diocesan parish, visitation, fábrica, tithe, and collación records;
- urban habices and apeos with linderos and parish or mosque associations;
- municipal acts involving feligresía, processions, disputes, taxation, streets,
  and terms; and
- historically resolved streets, properties, and toponyms that can control a
  textual boundary observation.

Each future observation must be classified as interior, exterior, contact, or
indeterminate. A boundary segment needs one direct documentary observation and
an independent spatial control. Conflicts remain as dated alternatives; missing
evidence remains a gap.

## Historical public-space alignments

The three spaces have separate evidence profiles:

- **Bib-Rambla:** municipal history supports Castilian enlargement and
  ceremonial use from 1501, but the fronts of 1550 have not been separated from
  later rectifications.
- **Campo del Príncipe:** institutional history supports the transformation of
  Campo de la Loma from 1497 and further works in 1513, but not identity with
  the present envelope.
- **Plaza Nueva:** municipal planning supports its sixteenth-century creation
  over the Darro, and the 1521 Darrillo document supplies a useful route and
  toponymic control. It does not specify the dimensions or sequence of bridges,
  platforms, and river coverings.

The evidence still required includes individual cabildo acts and works files,
property fronts and expropriations, dated archaeological plans, hydrological
controls for Plaza Nueva, and later maps whose deformations and persistent
control points can be published. A future envelope will be assembled by
independently sourced segments, not copied from the modern square.

## Population corpus

The dossier defines a record model before collecting observations. Required
provenance fields include source ID, archival locator, date, document class,
literal place, and spatial confidence. It separately records the historical
category actually used by the source, the actor's explicit role, the normalized
place when resolvable, analytical weight, and any exclusion reason.

The methodological constraints are substantive:

- a name, occupation, language, property, or address does not authorize an
  inferred religious or ethnic identity;
- resident, owner, tenant, taxpayer, institution, household, person, and
  property are distinct units;
- non-appearance is not evidence of absence, displacement, poverty, or
  confession;
- habices describe selected institutional property, not a census;
- fiscal and parish records must be documented by purpose, exclusions,
  denominator, and spatial coverage; and
- consequences after the 1568 revolt cannot be projected back into 1550.

The published Churriana de la Vega habices study is used only to understand the
record type and its potential fields. It is explicitly not evidence for the
urban distribution of Granada. No population layer can be considered until a
local sample proves transcription, linkage, duplicate handling, exclusions,
coverage reporting, and scale sensitivity and receives Morisco-history and
population-geography review.

## Composite citywide extent

A future citywide output must not collapse different phenomena into one line.
The protocol therefore keeps separate:

- built or physically occupied extent;
- defensive enclosure;
- ecclesiastical or administrative jurisdiction; and
- social observations from the population corpus.

The result can use only reviewed components with compatible dates, methods,
sources, and uncertainty. It must retain gaps, enclaves, orchards, peri-urban
areas, and competing edges. Every segment must retain lineage to its inputs,
and sensitivity tests must show which areas depend on a single class of
evidence.
